The bet: build the tool instead of reading it all by hand

Auditing scattered docs, tickets, and demo recordings by hand would have run weeks before a single friction point was named. I bet a week of build time against months of manual audit and vibe-coded a generator that synthesizes them into journey maps.

Three more calls, and where each one stands

Each turned on a fork where the obvious option was available and I took the other one. Where the data hasn't come back yet, the case study says so.

Structural change vs. familiarity: a navigation tradeoff

Research surfaced a better concept agents weren't sure they liked. I chose the structural fix over the familiar one, betting resistance would fade once agents adapted to it.

Data pending UX research · Usability testing

One dashboard or two: the personalization bet

The easy call was one shared dashboard for every agent. I pushed for role-based views instead, trading build complexity for relevance — a bet the data hasn't fully validated yet.

Why I didn't solve adoption with more training

Sellers weren't ignoring the platform for lack of training — it was friction. Instead of pushing more sessions, I built a guidebook that removed steps, betting less effort would beat more content.

Where the decisions get made

I'm a Denver-based product designer with 10+ years in design — at IBM as a copywriter, then a team lead, then a product owner and designer; at Transamerica in financial services; and most recently at Tovuti LMS, designing the interfaces that help teams teach, learn, and work together.

What connects all of it is a focus on the decisions that happen before anything gets built — the research, the framing, the systems thinking that makes the actual design work go faster and land better. I work upstream, where design decisions get made, and I translate between design, product, and engineering — and increasingly, between human intent and AI output.

Right now I'm spending most of my time at the intersection of AI and UX. Not as a trend, but as a real shift in how products get made and what designers are responsible for. I build practical tools for designers navigating that shift, published at erikftaylor.gumroad.com.
